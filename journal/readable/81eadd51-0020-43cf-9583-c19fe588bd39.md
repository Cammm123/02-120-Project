**Claude:** ## What `logLogFit` does

**The idea:** if a rate grows like a power of age, `rate = c × age^k`, then taking the log of both sides turns that curve into a straight line:

```
log(rate) = log(c) + k × log(age)
   y      = intercept + slope × x
```

The power k becomes the slope. So `logLogFit` takes logs of the ages and rates, then hands them to the `fitLine` you already built and tested. It adds nothing new except the logs and skipping groups that have 0 cases, since log(0) doesn't exist.

**Numbers you can check** (rate = age³, logs base 10):

| age | rate | log(age) | log(rate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 1,000 | 1.000 | 3.000 |
| 20 | 8,000 | 1.301 | 3.903 |

Slope = (3.903 − 3.000) / (1.301 − 1.000) = 0.903 / 0.301 = **3**. In plain words: doubling the age multiplies the rate by 2³ = 8.

```
 rate vs age (curves up)          log(rate) vs log(age) (straight)
 |                    *           |                  *
 |                 *              |              *
 |             *                  |          *
 |       *                        |      *
 |*  *                            |  *
 +-------------------- age        +-------------------- log(age)
```

## Power vs exponential

- **Power** (`rate = age³`): the rate multiplies by the same amount each time age *doubles*.
- **Exponential** (e.g. the rate doubles every 10 years): the rate multiplies by the same amount for every *fixed number of years added*.

Here's how much each one grows over 10 years, at a young age and an old age:

| 10-year step | age³ grows by | "doubles every 10 years" grows by |
|---|---|---|
| 20 → 30 | (30/20)³ = **3.4×** | **2×** |
| 70 → 80 | (80/70)³ = **1.5×** | **2×** |

Under the power law, the yearly percent growth slows down with age. Under the exponential, it stays the same. Exponential growth actually overtakes any power eventually, so the model is not "super exponential". It is the slower of the two.

Each kind turns straight on a different graph:
- power → straight on **log(rate) vs log(age)**: `logLogFit`, whose slope ≈ r − 1 hits
- exponential → straight on **log(rate) vs age**

So the log-log slope isn't just a readability trick. It's the measurement itself: it tells you how many hits a cell needs. A plain-axis graph can't give you that number.

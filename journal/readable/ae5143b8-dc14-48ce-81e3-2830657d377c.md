**Claude:** ## 1. R²: yes, mostly

R² is **the share of the up-and-down spread in the points that the line explains**. For the picture above: the points spread 2 around their mean, the line leaves 1.5 of that unexplained, so R² = 1 − 1.5/2 = **0.25**. The line explains only a quarter of the spread.

It says how well the line fits *these points*. It doesn't say whether it's the *right* line.

## 2. z-scores: close, but the scaling comes from the expected count

The noise in a count of random events is about **√(expected count)**. The number of people you ran affects z only because more people means bigger expected counts.

| expected | noise (√expected) | simulated | z |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | ±10 | 130 | 30 / 10 = **3** (unusual) |
| 10,000 | ±100 | 10,030 | 30 / 100 = **0.3** (normal) |

The miss is 30 in both rows. It matters in the first row and doesn't in the second.

## 3. The Binomial distribution, from the start

Take **one cell**. Every year it flips a weighted coin: a hit with chance p, a miss otherwise. After t years it has some number of hits between 0 and t. **The Binomial(t, p) distribution is the list of chances for each possible number of hits.**

Here's the smallest example, t = 3 years and p = 0.5 (a fair coin). All 8 equally likely histories:

```
MMM  → 0 hits         chance of 0 hits: 1/8
HMM  MHM  MMH → 1     chance of 1 hit:  3/8
HHM  HMH  MHH → 2     chance of 2 hits: 3/8
HHH  → 3              chance of 3 hits: 1/8
```

The model asks one thing: **has the cell reached r hits yet?** With r = 2: P(at least 2 hits) = 3/8 + 1/8 = **1/2**. So a one-cell person has a 1/2 chance of cancer by age 3, which means S(3) = 1/2. That's the number in spec hand check 5.

When p isn't 0.5, the histories aren't equally likely. The general formula counts the histories and weights each one:

  P(exactly k hits in t years) = C(t, k) · p^k · (1 − p)^(t − k)

`expectedRates` adds up these chances for k = r, r+1, …, t. That's the "sum the top directly" rule from the spec's arithmetic trap.

**Claude:** ## `fitLine`: what "correct" means

**In one sentence:** given points (x, y), `fitLine` returns the slope and intercept of the line with the **smallest sum of squared vertical misses**, plus R² = 1 − (sum of squared misses) / (spread of y around its mean).

In your projection language: A has a column of 1s and a column of x's. Solving AᵀA x̂ = Aᵀy gives:

  slope = Σ(x − x̄)(y − ȳ) / Σ(x − x̄)²    intercept = ȳ − slope · x̄

That's the same arithmetic as the spec's hand check.

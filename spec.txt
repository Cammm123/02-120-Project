CANCER AGE-CURVE SIMULATOR - BUILD SPEC (v1)
============================================
Status: DRAFT. All five decisions made 2026-10-09; waiting for a final read
  and OK. Nothing is built yet.
Created: 2026-10-09.


GOAL
----
Answer Q1 of proposal.md: can p, which hit a carcinogen speeds up, and when
exposure starts and stops produce the downward bend at old ages in lung cancer
in men (Armitage & Doll 1954, Fig. 3; CI5 England)?

v1 does NOT answer that yet. v1 only rebuilds the Armitage & Doll straight
line (no carcinogen) and proves it is right. Everything later is built on it.


THE MODEL (v1)
--------------
1. A person is a grid of N cells (default 10 x 10 = 100). Each cell holds a
   hit count from 0 to r. Everyone starts with 0 hits in every cell.

       one person, N = 9 cells, r = 3

       +---+---+---+
       | 0 | 1 | 0 |      each number = hits that cell has so far
       +---+---+---+
       | 2 | 0 | 0 |      a cell that reaches 3 = cancer
       +---+---+---+
       | 0 | 0 | 1 |
       +---+---+---+

   In v1 the cells do not affect each other, so the grid shape does not
   matter yet. It matters later, when cells divide and have neighbors.

2. Each year of age a = 0, 1, 2, ..., 84, every cell with fewer than r hits
   gets its NEXT hit with probability p. Hits come in a fixed order: a cell
   with 1 hit can only gain hit #2, never jump to hit #3.

       one cell, r = 3, p = 0.5 (made-up coin flips):

       age:     0     1     2     3     4
       flip:   miss  HIT   miss  HIT   HIT
       hits:    0     1     1     2     3   <- cancer at age 4

3. The first year any cell reaches r hits is the person's age at cancer.
   That person stops there (they are no longer cancer-free). Nobody reaches
   r by the end of age 84 -> age at cancer = none.

4. Simulate M people (default 20,000) and store a table with one row per
   person:  { person: 0, ageAtCancer: 61 },  { person: 1, ageAtCancer: null } ...

5. Cancer rate for each five-year age group (0-4, 5-9, ..., 80-84), using
   exact person-years (decided 2026-10-09):

       rate = (cases with ageAtCancer in the group)
              / (person-years at risk in the group)

   A person adds 1 person-year for each year of age in the group that they
   START cancer-free, including the year they get cancer. Example, group
   60-64: cancer-free all 5 years -> 5; cancer at 61 -> 2 (ages 60 and 61);
   cancer before 60 -> 0.
   This is cases per person-year, like CI5 (pop.csv counts person-years).
   (CI5 divides by everyone alive, including people who already had cancer;
   for a rare cancer the difference is tiny.)

6. Slope = the least-squares straight line through
   log(rate) vs log(middle age of the group), using the groups from 25-29 to
   80-84 (A&D used 25-74; CI5 goes to 85+). Groups with 0 cases are skipped,
   since log(0) does not exist.

7. Random numbers come from a seeded generator: the same seed gives the exact
   same people every time, so a test can be repeated. (JavaScript's built-in
   Math.random cannot be given a seed.)

8. Every run reports three numbers (decided 2026-10-09):
   - slope of the log-log line, next to the exact slope from the formula;
   - R squared of that line: how straight the points are (1 = perfectly
     straight). It does NOT say the slope is right: rates growing like age^2
     instead of age^3 still give R squared = 1. The bend in v2 should lower it.
   - a z-score for each age group: how far the simulated case count is from
     the expected count, in standard deviations:
         z = (simulated cases - expected cases) / sqrt(expected cases)
     Expected 100, got 130 -> z = 30 / 10 = 3.


THE EXACT ANSWER TO COMPARE AGAINST
-----------------------------------
With no carcinogen, a cell gets one yes/no chance per year, so after t years
its number of hits is min(r, Binomial(t, p)). That gives, with no simulation:

   P(one cell done by t years)  = P(Binomial(t, p) >= r)
   P(person cancer-free after t) = S(t) = (1 - P(cell done by t))^N
   expected rate in a group g..g+4 =
       (S(g) - S(g+5)) / (S(g) + S(g+1) + S(g+2) + S(g+3) + S(g+4))
   (top: share of people who get cancer in the group; bottom: expected
   person-years at risk, one year for each age they start cancer-free)

The simulation must land close to this (HAND CHECK 6). It is a stronger check
than "slope = r - 1", which is only true when hits are rare.

Computer-arithmetic trap (found 2026-10-09): with real numbers these chances
are tiny, and the obvious way to compute them is wrong.
  - P(Binomial(85, 1.4e-6) >= 6) computed as 1 - P(fewer than 6) gives
    3.3e-15; adding up P(exactly 6), P(exactly 7), ... directly gives the right
    answer, 3.29e-27. (A computer keeps only about 16 digits, so "1 minus
    something extremely close to 1" loses everything.)
  - (1 - q)^N with q = 1e-27 and N = 1e8 comes out as exactly 1 (so 0 risk),
    because 1 - 1e-27 rounds to 1. Use Math.log1p / Math.expm1, which are
    made for this.
expectedRates must sum the top of the Binomial directly and use log1p/expm1.


FUNCTIONS (built and tested one at a time, in this order)
---------------------------------------------------------
  makeRng(seed)                    -> a function giving numbers in [0, 1)
  stepCell(hits, r, p, rand)       -> the cell's hits after one year
  simulatePerson(N, r, p, rand)    -> age at cancer, or null
  simulatePopulation(M, N, r, p, seed) -> the table of people
  ageGroupRates(table)             -> 17 groups: cases, person-years, rate
  fitLine(xs, ys)                  -> slope, intercept and R squared of the
                                      least-squares line (plain numbers)
  logLogFit(ages, rates)           -> fitLine on log(age), log(rate),
                                      skipping groups with 0 cases
  expectedRates(N, r, p)           -> the exact rates from the formula above,
                                      plus expected cases per person
  zScores(simulated, expected, M)  -> one z per age group
  runReport(M, N, r, p, seed)      -> the three numbers for one run


HAND CHECKS (must pass)
-----------------------
All numbers below were worked out by hand and double-checked with a separate
calculation on 2026-10-09.

1. p = 0: nobody ever gets cancer (every ageAtCancer is null).

2. p = 1, r = 3: every cell gains a hit every year (ages 0, 1, 2), so every
   person gets cancer at age 2.

3. r = 1, N = 1 (one hit, proposal Test Case 3): the rate is flat. Each year
   a cancer-free person has chance p, so cases per person-year = p exactly.
   With p = 0.1: expectedRates gives 0.1 in EVERY group.

4. Line fitting.
   - logLogFit on rates made from rate = age^3: slope exactly 3, R squared 1.
   - A flat rate (age^0): slope 0.
   - Two points, rate 1 at age 10 and rate 4 at age 20:
     slope = log(4) / log(2) = 2.
   - fitLine on points (0, 0), (1, 2), (2, 1), worked by hand:
     means x = 1, y = 1; slope = [(-1)(-1) + 0 + (1)(0)] / [1 + 0 + 1] = 0.5;
     line gives 0.5, 1, 1.5; misses -0.5, 1, -0.5 -> squared 1.5;
     spread around the mean y: 1 + 1 + 0 = 2; R squared = 1 - 1.5/2 = 0.25.
   - zScores: expected 100, simulated 130 -> 3; simulated 100 -> 0.

5. expectedRates for a tiny case, N = 1, r = 2, p = 0.5 (a coin flip each
   year; cancer = the 2nd head). S(a) = P(at most 1 head in a flips):
     S(0) = 1, S(1) = 1, S(2) = 3/4, S(3) = 4/8 = 1/2, S(4) = 5/16,
     S(5) = 6/32 = 3/16.
   - Group 0-4: cases = 1 - 3/16 = 13/16.
     Person-years = 1 + 1 + 3/4 + 1/2 + 5/16 = 57/16.
     Rate = 13/57 = 0.22807.
   - Group 5-9 (needs S(5) ... S(10) = 11/1024): rate = 181/424 = 0.42689.

6. Simulation vs formula: with the default settings, the z-scores are small
   in every group. The pass line is SET BY MEASURING, not guessed: run 20
   seeds, look at how much the z-scores and the slope vary from run to run,
   and set the line at about 3 times that spread. Write the measured numbers
   here when we have them.

7. Slope vs r - 1 (proposal Test Cases 1 and 4): with r = 4, the slope is
   close to 3, but not equal, because hits are not perfectly rare:
     r = 4, p = 0.002 -> exact slope 2.99, but only about 56 cases in
                         20,000 people (too few to measure)
     r = 4, p = 0.005 -> exact slope 2.84, about 1,750 cases  <- default
   The simulated slope must be close to the exact slope (2.84; the pass line
   is measured as in check 6), and the gap from 3 is explained by the same
   flattening we measured in the tutor session (r = 7, p = 0.05: slopes
   4.0 -> 2.6).

8. Real numbers (option b, decided 2026-10-09). With about 1e8 cells per
   person (Luebeck & Moolgavkar 2002) and a real mutation rate of
   1.4e-6 per cell per year (their Table 1, APC gene, white males), the
   pure model's chance of cancer by age 85 is:
       r = 2: about 0.5        r = 4: 7.8e-10       r = 6: 3.3e-19
   So with 6 hits, nobody in 100,000 (or in the whole world) would get
   cancer, while real lifetime risks are a few percent. This is a known
   weakness of the pure multistage model, and the reason Luebeck &
   Moolgavkar add cells that divide. It goes in the final paper's discussion.
   How: expectedRates computes it from the formula (simulating 1e8 cells one
   by one is too slow, and would only ever show 0 cases). The test checks
   the r = 6 number, 3.3e-19, which needs the arithmetic trap above handled.
   (One p for every hit is a simplification: real hits are different
   genes with different rates.)


DEFAULTS (proposed)
-------------------
  N = 100 cells, r = 4 hits, p = 0.005 per year, seed = 1.
  M = 20,000 people for now (decided 2026-10-09); about 100,000 for the
  final versions. Rule for choosing M later: enough people that the 25-29
  group expects at least 100 cases (noise about +-10%), if it runs fast enough.
  Work: 20,000 x 100 x 85 = up to 170 million coin flips per run. Not timed
  yet; we measure it when simulatePopulation is built.
  Real cancers are far rarer; p is scaled up so the curve has enough cases
  (proposal Q2). Only the shape of the curve is compared with real data.


NOT IN v1 (later versions)
--------------------------
  v2: the carcinogen. It multiplies p for ONE chosen hit (#1 ... #r) between a
      start age and a stop age. This is what Q1 asks about, and what proposal
      Test Cases 2 (the 5,000x timing check) and 5 (the bend vs CI5) need.
  v3: the web page: sliders for p, which hit, start and stop; the log-log
      graph next to CI5 England lung cancer in men; presets.
  Later: cells that divide (Luebeck & Moolgavkar 2002), immune cells.
